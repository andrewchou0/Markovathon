Every prompt naming a supplier stays on the customer’s hardware

For defense programs the supplier graph is operating context worth protecting: local inference keeps relationships, stock, schedules, and program commitments on the box.

Access is about people, not regions

Controlled technical data restricts who may see it, not only where the server sits. A US region alone does not answer a person-based requirement.

Aggregation changes sensitivity

A part number, a supplier, a quantity, a date. Individually routine; compiled, they describe a program, its tempo, and its single points of failure.

Nothing to disclose, nothing to retain

A cloud call transmits the whole prompt: supplier names, stock positions, schedules, program references. Locally there is no vendor log, retention window, or subprocessor list to reason about.

Company-controlled operations

The full dependency graph and the context needed to choose a response stay close to the team that owns the decision. Cloud inference is not required at any point in the workflow.

Access controls, contractual duties, and export rules still apply. Local execution supports those controls.

What the rules ask, and what running locally changes

Local deployment is not a compliance certificate. It removes the part of the problem that is hardest to prove.

RequirementWhat it asks of the contractorWhat local deployment changes
CUI under DFARS 252.204-7012Implement NIST SP 800-171. A cloud service storing or processing CUI must meet FedRAMP Moderate equivalency.No external cloud service touches the records, so there is none to accredit.
CMMC assessmentEvidence those controls on every system that handles CUI, at the level the contract sets.A smaller system boundary is a smaller assessment surface.
ITAR technical dataThe 22 CFR 120.54 exclusion is conditional: end-to-end encryption, keys beyond foreign-person access.On-premises inference does not rely on that condition holding elsewhere.

Local does not prove compliance. It shrinks the boundary that has to be proven.

Neither DFARS nor ITAR bans cloud outright. Both place the burden of proof on the contractor.

For some of this work there is no cloud option to compare against

Air-gapped areas, deployed sites, and small subcontractors are not choosing local over cloud. Local is the only thing that runs.

No egress path exists

A closed area or a disconnected site has nowhere to send a prompt. A cloud agent is not slower there; it is absent. One box that runs offline is the only deployable form.

An enclave is a cost, not a checkbox

A primes-grade accredited environment is an annual expense. Many of the 12,000 small subcontractors cannot carry it. The hardware on their floor they already own.

A provider incident becomes your report

A third-party compromise touching your CUI is still your 72-hour report to DoD, and the clause flows that duty to every subcontractor. No third party, no inherited exposure.

Removing the third party removes the failure moderather than adding controls to manage it

DFARS 252.204-7012 requires reporting a cyber incident within 72 hours, and paragraph (m) flows that duty to subcontractors unaltered.
